# The Pipeline

These steps run in order, and each stage passes its buffer to the next. A helper named here as the "single source" for a per-channel value serves the CPU engine, the GPU engine and the curve chart.

**Color handling.** NegPy works on linear RGB straight from the raw decode (`output_color=raw`, `gamma=(1,1)`, unity white balance), with no input colorspace. Adobe RGB (1998) is the assumed boundary profile (`WORKING_COLOR_SPACE` in `infrastructure/display/color_spaces.py`): CLAHE, Lab and Toning compute CIELAB from linear data with its primaries and D65, and its TRC is the final engine step. Primaries apply only on output, in the display transform and the export conversion. Every render, preview, detection and thumbnail decode passes `adjust_maximum_thr=0.0`, which pins the scale to the camera's calibrated linearity limit (`user_sat`, else the format's white level), so a roll decodes on one scale and the preview matches export. Linear Output keeps the pin but not `user_sat`.

## 1. Geometry (Straighten & Crop)
**Code**: `negpy.features.geometry`

*   **Rotation**: 90° steps, then affine fine rotation with bilinear interpolation.
*   **Lens distortion**: a radial $k_1$ (`geometry.distortion_k1`, ±0.10), corrected in the same resample.
*   **Tilt and Swing** (`geometry.converge_v` / `converge_h`, ±15%): the projectivity of a tilted easel (Hartley & Zisserman §2.3). It runs last in the forward chain, after $k_1$, because a projectivity cannot fit a barrel-distorted frame. The unit is percent of the frame, convergence $(H/2)\sin\tau / D$. Output keeps the canvas size and replicates the wedge. A quarter-turn swaps the two axes and a flip negates the flipped one (`rotate_geometry_and_analysis`, `toggle_flip`).

    `keystone_matrix_normalized` is the one definition of the quad. The pixel matrix, the GPU inverse, the uv grid, `map_coords_to_geometry`, autocrop's replay and detection key, and the GPU analysis replay all derive from it. Off-frame card-edge handles need `CoordinateMapping` to fit the grid projectively.
*   **Crop Empty Corners** (`geometry.crop_to_valid`): the largest axis-aligned, centered rect that rotation and keystone leave void-free, used as the ROI when no crop is set. `compute_geometry_crop_rect` ternary-searches the half-width with a nested binary search for the half-height. Distortion is excluded, because it scales to fill.
*   **Square to Film** (`geometry.skew`): sets fine rotation, tilt and swing from the frame's straight film and gate edges, never the picture. A length-weighted mode of long segments squares the frame. Each side, searched inside the detector's box or its snap window, is fitted as a line, and Gauss-Newton levels the lines through the app's own warp. A correction needs box-led lines and an opposite pair (left/right for tilt, top/bottom for swing). A keystone is kept only between 0.3% and 5%, and only where it explains the edges clearly better than rotation alone.
*   **Autocrop**: finds the film edge from the density jump to the scanner bed. Detection runs once per edit in `ImageProcessor`, ahead of both engines, and stores the rect (`geometry.crop_rect`, origin in `crop_from_auto`) with its detection key (`autocrop_detection_key`: orientation, ratio, mode, rebate trim). A key change re-detects. Both engines only slice that rect, because a preview and a full-res export can stop on different edges. Crop Offset is not in the key; every render re-applies it. **Square First** (`autocrop_square`) runs the Square to Film fit (`skew.squared_geometry`) once per arm on a frame without its own skew and detects on the squared geometry.

    The surround of a box must read as bed (uniform, near-clipping) or as a dark holder, else detection falls back to the full frame. Opaque edges are trimmed off the box, up to 20% a side; a run that uses the whole allowance trims nothing. A box under a quarter of the frame is not film. On `Free` the ratio comes from the detected frame, snapped to the nearest real film format.
*   **Rebate Trim** (*Image only*): refines the film box inward to the exposed image by one of three routes. A uniformity probe works when bed, rebate and image tiers separate. A per-side edge walk (`measure_film_edges`) serves camera scans with thin rebates: a 3% pass, and a 10% pass adopted only when it is deeper and passes four consistency tests. A per-side ring measurement (`measure_film_border`) needs an opposite pair, because a bright sky at the frame edge reads like film base. 0 stops at the film edge, 1.0 at the image edge, and above 1.0 cuts into the picture. Crop All Frames scales its roll-pooled inset by the same factor, each side pooling on its own sample count. In a roll, a portrait frame resolves from its own crop, a frame with no film box is placed from the roll, and deskew is not pooled.

The crop selection becomes the analysis ROI. The pixels are cropped only after Toning, so Lab and the preview overlays see the full frame. The Analysis Buffer excludes the outer X%, and a freehand region (`analysis_rect`) sets exactly what the meters read.

A frame with no crop meters a **meter rect** (`process.meter_rect`, `resolve_meter_rect`): the rect Auto Crop would keep, but only when the film sits inside an opaque holder or on bare light, because a holder is denser than any film and would take the print's white point. `ImageProcessor` resolves it once ahead of both engines, frozen with `meter_detection_key` (the detection key plus the source's aspect). A new rect drops per-frame bounds measured without it. Roll Analysis reads the same rect.

## 2. Scan Normalization
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure.normalization`

Pixel values are linear transmittance. The source corrections run in that domain, before the log.

*   **Flat-field** (`negpy.features.flatfield`): a per-channel gain $\text{mean}(\text{blur})/\text{blur}$ from a blank reference, computed on a 256 px copy with $\sigma$ = 1/64 of the long side, clamped to $[0.25, 4]$. The blur and mean are a normalized convolution over the lit area (pixels above 0.35 of the 95th-percentile luminance), so a dark border does not overcorrect the frame beside it. The gain is stored in sensor layout and turned by each frame's decode orientation. It is baked once into a profile (an `.npz` in `APP_CONFIG.flatfield_dir`, keyed by an opaque id, with a 512 px float16 copy of the reference for the self-check: Check Flat Field shows that copy times the gain, each channel over its lit-area median, ±10% to black and white, or 1/gain over ±15% without the copy); the edit stores only the id. The render resolves it through `set_gain_provider` (wired to `services/assets/flatfield.py`); the profile id and a gain content token fold into the source hash.
*   **Embedded lens correction** (`negpy.features.lens`): the camera's own distortion and lateral CA (DNG `WarpRectilinear`, Sony ARW; Panasonic RW2 distortion only, after darktable's reader), applied at decode after flat-field and before the unmix, so neither engine needs a shader. Composites and RGB+IR sources skip it. A correction that reads past the frame edge is scaled to fill (`fill_scale` ≤ 1).
*   **Sensor crosstalk unmix** (`sensor_matrix`, `features/process/sensor.py`): for single-exposure narrowband camera scans, whose CFA passbands overlap the light's bands. The matrix is calibrated from three bare-light exposures (columns normalized to a unit diagonal), inverted, and applied to the linear capture. Where the film passes almost none of a band, the subtraction falls below grain and calibration error, which the log turns into maximum density. `sensor_unmix` picks the handling (default `two_scale`):
    *   `linear` clips at zero.
    *   `density` applies $C = \mathrm{diag}(1/(M b))\,M\,\mathrm{diag}(b)$, the unmix linearized at the film base $b$ (rows sum to 1), to $\ln x - \ln b$, holding each channel at $10^{-3} b_c$ or above.
    *   `two_scale` is the clipped linear unmix while the own-channel gain $M_{cc}\bar x_c / (M\bar x)_c$ stays below 1.15 $C_{cc}$, blending by a smoothstep (full at 1.5 $C_{cc}$) toward $\mathrm{SF}(M\bar x)\,\exp\!\big(C(\ln x - \ln \bar x)\big)$. $\bar x$ is the capture blurred by $\sigma = $ long edge / 1200, and $\mathrm{SF}$ the smooth maximum $\tfrac12\big(u + f + \sqrt{(u - f)^2 + f^2}\big)$ with $f = 0.15\,M_{cc}\bar x_c$. A channel whose detail $\ln(x_c/\bar x_c)$ sits $\ln 64$ below both others is a dead photosite and takes their mean.

    $b$ is the median color of the interior cells thinnest in all three channels. A channel whose 99.5th percentile sits at its maximum is clipped, and both methods fall back to `linear`. A stitch is unmixed once, then assembled; a half-frame is sliced after. `unmix_block_reason` refuses the unmix on a transparency and on a camera-WB decode (`linear_raw` off), where a diagonal gain does not commute with it.
*   **HDR merge** (`features/hdr`): a bracket becomes one linear source at decode, with no shader. The reference is the longest exposure with a negligible clipped fraction (`choose_reference`), and the merge is in its units, so it stays in $[0, 1]$. It is cached unscaled, with values above 1.0; `apply_render_exposure` applies the render exposure (`output_scale` ≤ 1, from the user's `hdr_anchor`, else the bracket's median log ratio) before the final clip, so an exposure change needs no new decode. `hdr_merge_token` excludes the exposure; `hdr_token` includes it. A fresh merge is seeded with a Shadows Density lift (`seed_shadow_density`, $-0.30$ per stop of `shadow_reach_stops`, capped at $-0.70$).

    Ratios are solved from the images: `pair_ratio` is the median per-sample ratio over samples above the noise floor in the shorter frame and unsaturated in the longer, chained from the reference between exposure-adjacent frames (`exposure_order_key`: clipped fraction, then level). Samples combine by inverse variance (weight $r^2$), rolling off to zero between $0.90$ and $0.995$ per channel, into float32. The merge runs before flat-field and the unmix, because a gain map moves the saturation point the weights key on. Every frame decodes on the reference frame's white balance (`HdrWorker.run` passes the first frame's multipliers), because per-frame multipliers make `pair_ratio` solve wrong ratios.
*   **Composite assembly** (`features/rgbscan`, `features/stitch`): runs before all of the above. A Trichrome triplet takes each channel from the exposure lit by that band, green and blue registered to red by phase correlation, on a neutral white balance with no crosstalk unmix. A stitch decodes each part, warps it by the registration fixed at stitch time, matches per-channel gain, and cuts a minimum-error seam with a narrow cross-fade, because averaging the whole overlap draws misregistered detail twice. The IR defect mask follows the cut. Flat-field and unmix apply per part, never to the canvas.
*   **Log conversion**: $E_{log} = \log_{10}(I_{raw})$.
*   **Bounds**: percentiles find the usable range; the White Point maps to the Floor ($0.0$) and the Black Point to the Ceiling ($1.0$). On a negative, dense highlights map to the Floor and film base to the Ceiling, over 0.01% to 99.99%. Transparency takes no metering (§3, *Transparency transfer*).

    `_sample_log_bounds` samples two independent axes. A color pass reads each channel's floor and ceiling references; a luma pass sets how far the window reaches past them, as one shared fraction of each channel's span $s_c$: $f_c = r_c^f + (h_f + k)\,s_c$, $h_f = \sum_c (l_c^f - r_c^f) / \sum_c s_c$, and the ceiling alike. A film whose channels differ in range keeps a gray scale neutral. $k$ is the base's mean-minus-median channel offset over the mean span. Spans of mixed sign take one log reach for all channels.
    *   **Luma Range Clip** (`luma_range_clip`): the luminance window. Positive tightens it; zero uses outlier-resistant extremes (a block-median prefilter and a small base clip); negative pushes the bounds outward as headroom.
    *   **Color Clip** (`color_range_clip`): the sampling depth for per-channel deviation, neutral at `base_color_clip` ($1.0$), log-interpolated either side. The thin end uses per-channel percentiles. The dense end reads one shared, chroma-gated set (the lowest-chroma part of the luma-extreme band), so colored highlights cannot pass as film cast; without trustworthy neutrals, and always in Transparency, it uses per-channel percentiles.
    *   **White and Black Point**: offsets on the detected bounds, plus per-layer trims (`white_point_trim_*` / `black_point_trim_*`; `per_channel_point_offsets`, single source; Transparency negates).
*   **Roll baseline and locks**: Roll Analysis measures every frame with its own crop, region and crosstalk. A frame whose luma-free color offsets lie further than `POOL_OUTLIER_DISTANCE` log density from the median is an outlier and keeps its own bounds on every apply of the baseline. The baseline pools the rest: luma as the median of luma-weighted floors and ceilings, color as the mean offset. `use_luma_average` / `use_color_average` apply it per axis; `lock_bounds` exempts a frame. A scene pools and writes only its members, and the roll baseline skips them.

    A frame saves its bounds with the edit. Bounds saved under an older `BOUNDS_VERSION` are dropped at startup (except under `lock_bounds`) and when a sidecar is read; a roll or scene baseline is flagged instead until its analysis runs again.

    The analysis also pools each inlier's Cast Removal neutral axis by a confidence-weighted median per band and channel (`pool_neutral_axis`); the highlight band pools only when half the frames have one. `use_cast_average` (Color Negative) renders with that axis (`locked_neutral_axis`) on both engines: the pool's R/B-vs-G curve, with the midtone level moved toward the frame's own by `weight × confidence × strength` (`blend_neutral_axis`). The strength is the Frontier's cast-hue table (`ez171autoc-nega.dat` 0x4D18), piecewise linear in the hue $\operatorname{atan2}(B - \frac{R+G}{2}, \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(R - G))$ of the offset in negative density: $0$ for red-magenta to magenta, $0.3$ for green, $0.6$ for yellow to red, $1$ for blue to azure. The weight is $1 - \sigma^2_{noise}/\sigma^2_{offset}$, the spread of midtone offsets against meter noise (half the spread of shadow-minus-midtone offsets), and zero below three frames.

    Detect Scenes runs the same per-frame pass and groups the frames by light instead (`detect_light_groups`). A frame's light is its midtone R/B offset from the pool's curve at its own green, so the subject's colors do not count; an axis under confidence `SCENE_MIN_CONFIDENCE` ($0.2$) joins no group. The scan order counts by its evidence $e = 1 - \tilde d_{adj}/\tilde d_{all}$, clipped to $[0, 1]$, the median offset distance of neighbors against that of all pairs: each offset is averaged with its neighbors 1, 2 and 3 frames away (weights `SCENE_ORDER_REACH` × $e$) that are under the same light, so strips scanned out of order get no weight. Complete linkage then groups frames whose every pair is within `SCENE_LIGHT_DISTANCE` ($0.08$ log D). The largest group stays with the roll baseline. Each other group keeps only frames whose own light is within `SCENE_MEMBER_RADIUS` ($0.05$ log D) of the group's median, so an unsure frame stays out of the scene's pool. Each group of two or more frames that remains waits for Accept, which makes it a scene and runs Scene Analysis on it.
*   **Stretch**: each channel stretches independently to $[0, 1]$, which removes the orange mask and base tints. The result is not clamped; the print curve's knees roll off tones outside the bounds.
*   **Per-frame metering**: an exposure anchor (Auto Density) and a textural range ($P_{10}$ to $P_{90}$ luminance, Auto Grade), read on textured cells only. Sectors of 2×2 `activity_block` cells vote when their block means span more than `activity_gate_density` ($0.05$ log D; Boyack & Juenger, US 5,724,456); below `activity_min_fraction` passing sectors, every cell votes.
*   **Channel unmix / crosstalk** (`crosstalk_strength` / `crosstalk_matrix`, docs/CROSSTALK.md): a 3×3 unmix of the negative log densities before bounds and stretch, where dye absorptions, light spectrum and CFA are linear, so a profile describes a scanning setup. It is blended with identity by strength and row-normalized, so grays stay. Every meter reads the unmixed film, so a change needs a new Roll Analysis.
*   **Density above base**: `density_above_base` is (Dmin, Dmed, Dmax) above the clear base, luminance-weighted: the thin and dense bounds (before trims; the roll's under Roll Analysis) and the metered median, each minus the base. The base is the roll's picked one (`rolls.roll_film_base`, carried as `film_base`), else the rebate reader's, unmixed like the meters. The reader takes the flat 8-px blocks (std over mean under 0.03, unclipped) in the outer 15% of the film box (`film_and_picture` at `AUTOCROP_DETECT_RES`), groups them by brightness with a 0.04-decade gap, and takes the largest group of at least five. It finds none without a film box, with a base darker than the picture, or on the transfer path. The preview reads it once per source, on the flat-fielded, unmixed source before the defect repairs, and passes the read to both engines.
*   **Scan-clip warning**: `scan_clip_fractions` reports the per-channel fraction of source pixels at or above $0.99$ linear. On a negative this clips film base and shadows to $D=0$; nothing reconstructs it.

## 3. The Print (Exposure)
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure`

*   **B&W Negative**: the normalized signal collapses to one density (its luminance) before the curve.
*   **Color timing**: subtractive CMY in log space, on Global, Shadows or Highlights. Regional offsets weigh by $w_{sh} = \sigma(3 \cdot (v - z))$ about the midtone zone center $z$ (`anchor_target_density`). Temperature, the WB picker and the temperature roll-lock act on the selected region's M/Y pair.
*   **The H&D curve**: a toe-linear-shoulder curve in density. A line of slope $k$ through the pivot is bounded by two independent softplus knees. With $v = k \cdot (x_{adj} - x_0)$:
    $$v_1 = D_{min} + \frac{\text{softplus}\big(a_{hl} (v - D_{min})\big)}{a_{hl}} \qquad \text{(shoulder → paper white)}$$
    $$D = D_{max} - \frac{\text{softplus}\big(a_{sh} (D_{max} - v_1)\big)}{a_{sh}} \qquad \text{(toe → paper black)}$$
    $D_{min} = 0.06$ (0 with `paper_dmin` off), $D_{max} = 2.3$. The knee sharpness $a_{sh}, a_{hl}$ comes from `toe_sharpness_base` ($6.0$) and `shoulder_sharpness_base` ($3.0$), scaled by `toeshoulder_width_ref` / width. $k$ is the per-channel slope from Grade, $x_{adj}$ the log-exposure after CMY offsets, $x_0$ the pivot.
*   **Preflash** (`preflash`, 0 to 1 of the threshold exposure): a uniform exposure on the straight line, before any shaping: $v \leftarrow v_{th} + \gamma \log_{10}\big(10^{(v - v_{th})/\gamma} + f\big)$, with $\gamma = 2.9 / (R/100)$ the paper's density per log exposure at the frame's ISO R, and $v_{th}$ the value that prints `preflash_threshold_density` ($0.04$) above $D_{min}$ (`preflash_params`). It is a paper exposure, so the scan's log range does not change it. It follows dodge/burn, and highlight hold measures after it.
*   **Paper S-curve**: before the bounds, $v \mathrel{+}= \gamma \cdot w \cdot \tanh\big((v - v^{\ast})/w\big)$ (`paper_midtone_gamma` $= 0.05$, `paper_gamma_width` $= 0.6$) about the reference tone $v^{\ast}$. Snap (`midtone_gamma`) trims $\gamma$, with per-layer trims (`midtone_gamma_trim_*`; `per_channel_midtone_gamma`, single source).
*   **Grade (ISO-R)**: default 115, range 50 to 180; R110 is about paper grade 2, higher R is softer. $k = 2.9 \cdot \text{range} / (R/100)$ (`grade_contrast_scale`), clamped to $[2.0, 10.0]$.
*   **Split Grade** (`shadow_grade` / `highlight_grade`, ISO-R points, negative harder): rotates the curve about the zone centers, $v \mathrel{+}= \Delta k_{ch} \cdot w \cdot (v - z_{zone})$, with the Zone Density weights below (`split_grade_deltas`, single source; per-layer `shadow_grade_trim_*` / `highlight_grade_trim_*`). It runs before Zone Density as its own block, because sequential blocks stay monotone and shared weights do not.
*   **Per-layer trims** (crossover correction): CMY only shifts a layer's curve; these change its shape.
    *   **Grade trim** (`grade_trim_*`, ±30 ISO-R points): the slope ratio $R/(R+\Delta R)$, like a paper's `channel_gamma`. The pivot is re-solved per channel, so midtones stay neutral.
    *   **Toe / Shoulder trims** (`toe_trim_*` / `shoulder_trim_*`, ±1; `per_channel_toe_shoulder`), **Snap trim** (±0.5) and **Width trims** (`toe_width_trim_*` / `shoulder_width_trim_*`, ±2, effective values in [0.1, 5]; `per_channel_widths`).
*   **Toe and Shoulder**: slider values scaled by $0.85$, per channel. The slider sets roll-off height, the width sets sharpness. Toe lifts the paper-black ceiling, $D_{max,eff} = D_{max} - \text{toe} \cdot 0.90$ (`toe_height`); a negative toe sharpens the knee and, with Paper Black off, raises the BPC clip point. Shoulder lifts the paper-white floor, $D_{min,eff} = D_{min} + \text{shoulder} \cdot 0.35$ (`shoulder_height`). Hard grades get snappier toes and compressed shoulders by the normalized slope (`toe_grade_strength` $\approx 0.058$, `shoulder_grade_strength` $= 0.12$).
*   **Zone Density** (`shadow_density` / `highlight_density`, ±1.0 ΔD): achromatic offsets with mid-sparing weights, $v \mathrel{+}= \Delta D_{sh} \cdot \sigma\big(k(v - z_{sh})\big) + \Delta D_{hl} \cdot \big(1 - \sigma(k(v - z_{hl}))\big)$, $z_{sh} = z + 0.75$, $z_{hl} = z - 0.40$, $k = 4$ (`zone_density_*`, literals in `exposure.wgsl`). It runs before the softplus bounds, so a burn never passes paper black and a bleach never crosses paper white. The chart mirrors it (`CharacteristicCurve`).
    *   **Hold Black** (`hold_black`): on a lift, the shadow weight is multiplied by a smoothstep from 1 at $D_{max,eff} - \tau$ to 0 at $D_{max,eff}$ (`zone_black_hold_taper` $\tau = 0.6$, per channel), so the black point stays and the curve stays monotone. A burn is unchanged.
*   **Dodge & Burn** (`negpy.features.local`): masks with an exposure in stops (`LocalMask.stops`, ±2; positive burns) and a Gaussian feather (fraction of the short side), rasterized to a stop map added to the log exposure with the CMY offsets. One stop is $\log_{10}(2)$ scaled by each channel's stretch range (`local_ev_scale`). Vertices are raw-image coordinates through `map_coords_to_geometry`. The Flat intent skips them.
    *   **Shapes** (`LocalMask.shape`): *Polygon*, N points, closed and Catmull-Rom smoothed (`smooth_polyline`). *Oval*, 3 points $(c, p_1, p_2)$, outline $c + u\cos\theta + v\sin\theta$ over 64 samples, $u = p_1 - c$, $v = p_2 - c$. *Gradient*, 2 points $(a, b)$, alpha $= 1 - \text{smoothstep}(t)$, $t = \big((p-a)\cdot d\big)/|d|^2$ clamped, $d = b - a$, no feather. `invert` gives $1 - \alpha$ after the feather; a disabled mask is skipped.
    *   One function (`local/logic.rasterise`) serves the render, the canvas tint and the printing notes. The GPU uses the same CPU map (`compute_local_maps` → the dodge/burn texture), so shapes have no parity surface.
    *   **Local Grade** (`LocalMask.grade`): plane 1 sums $\Delta R$ into a per-pixel slope factor $R/(R+\Delta R)$, clamped to the ISO-R ladder (`local_grade_factor_map`, mirrored in `exposure.wgsl`). It multiplies the straight-line slope only, $v = k \cdot g \cdot (x_{adj} - x_0) + c \cdot x_{adj}^2$, about the channel pivot; the cast curvature $c$ stays global. On the GPU it rides the texture's blue channel, so a Grade change uploads no map.
    *   **Local Flash** (`LocalMask.flash`): plane 2 sums $f_i \alpha_i$, added to the frame's preflash $f$ (green channel on the GPU). A tone-limited mask carries no flash.
    *   **Tone limit** (`key`, `key_zone`, `key_softness`; Kodak O-3): weight $\alpha \cdot s(t)$, $t = (L - e_0)/(e_1 - e_0)$ clamped, $s = t^2(3-2t)$ (`tone_key_weight`). $L$ is the luma of the unburned normalized log, so no mask moves the pixels it selects. The edges invert the achromatic print curve at $Z \mp S/2$ (`key_edges`). The first four limited masks (`MAX_KEYED_MASKS`) keep their own shape planes; a fifth prints unlimited.
*   **Contrast Mask** (`contrast_mask`, ±0.5): a blurred low-gamma mask sandwiched with the negative, $D' = D - g\,\text{blur}(D) + \text{const}$ (Ctein, *Post Exposure*). Positive scales the low-frequency range by $(1-g)$, negative by $(1+g)$; detail above the blur scale passes at unity. `contrast_mask_plane` builds a zero-mean plane of print stops, read with the dodge/burn map, on the same pre-geometry array in both engines. The slider is a scalar on it (`contrast_mask_scale`); the CPU caches the plane at render size (`expand_mask_plane`), the GPU on the analysis grid. **Mask Spacer** (`mask_spacer`, 2 to 6%, default 4) is the blur $\sigma$ as a percent of the analysis grid and keys both engines' plane caches, including the plane a tiled export shares. The plane covers the printed frame only, edge-replicated outside the crop. Instruments read the unmasked negative.
*   **Diffusion** (`diffusion`, 0 to 1): a diffuser at the lens, $I' = (1-d)\,I + d\,\text{blur}_\sigma(I)$, a mix of light, $\sigma$ = `DIFFUSION_SPREAD` (2% of the frame's short side), $d$ half the slider (`diffusion_mix`). Per channel $v \leftarrow \log_{10}\big((1-d)\,10^{vr} + d\,10^{pr}\big)/r$, with $r$ the channel's log range (`channel_density_ranges`). `diffusion_grid` blurs the unmixed light on the Contrast Mask's analysis grid, and `diffusion_plane` applies the final bounds. It runs before filtration and dodge/burn, which commute with it. Both engines sample the grid bilinearly (`plane_taps`); on the GPU it rides the Contrast Mask texture. Print curve only.
*   **Output**: $I_{out} = 10^{-D}$, scene-linear.
    *   **Paper Black** (`paper_black`): off applies black point compensation, $I_{out} = (I - t_b) / (1 - t_b)$ clamped at 0, $t_b = 10^{-D_b}$, with $D_b$ the physical $D_{max}$, or $D_{max} + \text{toe}_{ch} \cdot 0.90$ for a negative toe, which makes exact 0 reachable. The reference is the physical $D_{max}$, not $D_{max,eff}$, so a lifted toe survives. On keeps the paper's $10^{-2.3}$.
    *   **Scene-linear internally**: every creative stage works on linear light, and the Adobe RGB TRC (a pure $563/256 \approx 2.199$ power) is the final engine step. Retouch is perceptual: the CPU brackets it through the OETF; the GPU keeps one encoded region (exposure → clahe/retouch encoded → lab decodes).
    *   **Soft proof**: a $65^3$ color table (`soft_proof_lut` over `soft_proof_preview`), sampled by the canvas shader (`apply_lut_f32` on the CPU display path), with no parity surface. `ProofCondition` holds intent, compensation, paper white, ink black and gamut warning as one hashable value keying the table and the render memo.
    *   **Printability**: both engines bin encoded content into a $32^3$ RGB histogram (`COLOR_HIST_BINS`). The CPU gamut mask round-trips the identity grid source → output → source (`ImageProcessor.gamut_lut`); its tolerance sits above 8-bit noise, so it under-reports at the boundary.
    *   **Waveform**: encoded luma in 128 bins for each of 256 columns (`WAVEFORM_BINS`, `WAVEFORM_COLS`), with each cell's mean color (`WAVEFORM_COLOR_SCALE`) and a per-channel parade. The density pass bins scan density per crop column for the scan Parade (`SCAN_DENSITY_RANGE`, inverting the stretch) and counts linear pixels at `scan_clip_level` before the unmix. The base line is `normalization.base_scan_density`.
    *   **Vectorscope**: CIELAB a\*b\* in 180 hue × 64 chroma bins, log chroma with a knee at 4, last bin past chroma 60 (`SCOPE_*`).
    *   **Input ICC**: overrides primaries only. A matrix/TRC profile contributes its primaries (Bradford-adapted to D65 through its `chad` tag) as a plain matrix, so its TRC is inert. A LUT (A2B0/B2A0) profile runs the full CMS transform.

### Automatic helpers

*   **Auto Density** (`auto_exposure`, on): the metered tone $m$ averages the trimmed window's mean and midpoint ($P_5$ to $P_{95}$ of textured cells; Boyack & Juenger). The anchor is a partial pull from `assumed_anchor` ($0.46$):
    $$\text{anchor} = \text{assumed} + s \cdot (m - \text{assumed}), \quad \text{clamped to } \pm b$$
    with $s$ = `anchor_meter_strength` ($0.2$), $b$ = `anchor_meter_band` ($0.12$). It prints at `anchor_target_density` ($0.75$).
*   **Auto Grade** (`auto_normalize_contrast`, on): a partial ISO R match (Alkofer, US 4,731,671) on the textural range $t$ ($P_{10}$ to $P_{90}$, log D). With $r$ the floor-to-ceil range, the effective range is
    $$K \cdot r \cdot \min\!\Big((1 - s) + s \cdot \frac{n}{t},\; c \cdot \frac{n}{t}\Big)$$
    $K$ = `auto_grade_target` ($0.85$), $n$ = `auto_grade_nominal_range` ($0.9$), $s$ = `auto_grade_strength` ($0.4$), $c$ = `auto_grade_max_overfill` ($1.2$).
    *   **Shadow reach** (Gindele, US 7,113,649): the darkest textured tone ($P_{99}$) must print at least `shadow_reach_density` ($1.9$, straight-line); a short grade is steepened, never flattened.
    *   **Highlight hold** (Agfa, US 4,104,069): the brightest textured tone ($P_2$) must print at least `highlight_hold_density` ($0.10$; 0 off). Otherwise an automatic burn joins the Zone Density highlight term, capped at `highlight_hold_max` ($0.5$). Highlights Grade is not the actuator, because it pivots where these tones sit.
    *   **Shadow hold**: the Zone Density shadow term gets $-\min\big(h \cdot (v - v_r - o),\; m\big)$ when the tail's value $v > v_r + o$, with $v_r$ the value that prints `shadow_reach_density`, $h$ = `shadow_hold_strength` ($0.40$; 0 off), $o$ = `shadow_hold_overshoot` ($0.15$), $m$ = `shadow_hold_max` ($0.3$).
    *   All three aim through the midtone gamma (the stock's plus Snap). Shadow reach and the preflash threshold invert it, and the holds read the tail's $v$ after it, where the Zone Density terms add.
*   **On the sliders** (`features/exposure/auto_sliders.py`; transfer twin in `features/transparency/logic.py`): stored values are offsets on the meter. Density shifts by $(\text{anchor} - \text{assumed})/$`density_multiplier`, Grade is the ISO R of the auto slope on the frame's range (`slope_to_grade`), and the zone densities add the holds. The meters come from the frame's last plain render (`desktop/auto_sliders.py`).
*   **Set Targets**: eight app-global aim values (`TUNABLE_TARGETS`), overlaid on `EXPOSURE_CONSTANTS` by `apply_targets()` and saved as `exposure_targets`. No `WorkspaceConfig` hash sees them, so both engines fold `TARGETS_REVISION` into their cache keys (CPU base stage; GPU analysis key and exposure diff). A target read outside those stages needs its own invalidation.
*   **Cast Removal** (`cast_removal_strength`; 1.0 on a negative, 0 on a slide): balances each layer against the frame's grays from shadows to highlights, at `confidence × slider` (`effective_cast_strength`). `cast_removal_for_mode` is the one place the per-mode default swap lives; it rewrites only the other mode's default, so a chosen strength survives a mode switch. It is a roll default.

    The neutral axis meter reads what the curve consumes: the unmixed film log on the print curve, the working log after the camera matrix on the transfer curve (`needs_axis` in `gpu_engine.py`). `use_cast_average` swaps in the roll or scene axis (§2). The solve fits R and B to green's axis from highlight, midtone and shadow references, each over its band's lowest-chroma pixels. On the print curve a cell is dropped first when its offset is above `neutral_axis_foliage_chroma` ($0.03$) at a hue from green to yellow ($-25°$ to $80°$ of $\operatorname{atan2}(-s, t)$, $t$ and $s$ the mean and half-difference of R−G and B−G), or above `neutral_axis_sky_chroma` ($0.06$) from azure to blue ($-150°$ to $-60°$), because in a frame with no grays they print magenta or yellow. Confidence falls as the interdecile width passes `neutral_axis_width_deadzone`. R and B get a quadratic through the three points, midtone pinned through the pivot, offset clamped to $\pm 0.2$ (`midtone_cast_max_offset`), curvature to `neutral_axis_curv_max_ratio` ($0.45$ of the slope), and held at its vertex outside $[0,1]$ (`quadratic_core`), so far values such as the carrier's rebate ramp never fold back. Without trustworthy near-neutrals there is no correction.

    The transfer curve has no per-channel slope, so it uses an affine on density (`neutral_axis_affine`) landing each channel's midtone and shadow refs on green's, before every other control, with the same clamp and the gain bounded by `cast_affine_gain_limit` ($2.0$ and its reciprocal).

### Print stocks
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure.papers`

A print stock (`paper_profile`, default *Digital*) sets the curve shape without touching contrast or exposure: $D_{max}/D_{min}$, knee sharpness and height, midtone gamma. Color stocks add a slope crossover (`channel_gamma`), a base tint (`base_tint_cmy`) and dyes (`print_color`). *Digital* and the B&W papers have an identity `DyeMatrix`, so *Digital* reproduces the defaults exactly.

A color stock's dyes are a $33^3$ LUT (`DyeLut`, `features/exposure/print_luts/<key>.npz`) from dye density above base $e = D - D_{min}$ to linear Adobe RGB, media-relative, tetrahedral: $T = 10^{-D_{min}} \cdot \mathrm{LUT}(e)$ replaces $10^{-D}$. On its gray axis $Y(\mathrm{LUT}(e, e, e)) = 10^{-e}$, so tone tools stay true and grays drift only in chroma. Black point compensation maps the black's luminance to 0 with one scalar, so a tinted black keeps its tint. `scripts/build_print_luts.py` bakes the LUTs: E-4070, E-4021, H-1-2383 and H-1-2393t from the datasheets' spectral dye densities (CIE 1931 2°, D50 for paper, a Kinoton 75P xenon projector for print film, Bradford to D65; the E-4021 and 2393 curves are spektrafilm's traces, CC BY-SA 4.0, `NOTICE.md`); the Crystal Archive stocks from Fujifilm's Frontier-PD printer profiles, whose gray axis gives one curve per dye; *Digital (Pakon)* from `tests/fixtures/RGBScan.icc` applied to a Digital print, exact at the defaults; *Digital (Frontier)* from a Frontier 350/370's file output (`--frontier-dir`): its 10-bit print density through the negative's Pass1 matrix (rows normalized), `ColorData.dat`'s tone curves (stored G, R, B) and $32^3$ LUT (its chroma blurred by two nodes, `FRONTIER_CUBE_BLUR`, because its 8-bit nodes turn some hues within one step and trilinear lookups draw that as corners), read as sRGB because the machine writes and shows that F-Color as is, with the gray axis solved as for a Frontier-PD profile.

Profiles are mode-aware: Color Negative lists the color stocks, B&W Negative the B&W papers (with `lith_path` for Lith), Transparency only *Digital*; an incompatible value collapses to *Digital*. The Kodak curves are fitted to their datasheets (`scripts/fit_paper_profile.py`) at the slope the Grade gives each ISO R. Crystal Archive takes its knees from a fit to koraks.nl's measured gray wedge, its midtone from Endura Premier, and its $D_{min}$, tint and $D_{max}$ from the Frontier-PD profile. *Digital (Frontier)* takes its curve shape from a fit to its tables' gray curve on the print density axis, 543 codes per log E (green's scale through the negative LUTs). Its `slope_gain` (1.2) multiplies the slope the Grade gives, in `grade_to_slope` and its inverse, before the `slope_max` clamp, so L\* 90 to 10 spans the same log exposure as on *Digital*. The gain that matches ISO R instead (1.5) also multiplies the density differences between the dye layers, and warm shadows then reach black in magenta and yellow while cyan still moves. 2383 and 2393 are fitted at their steepest gamma, layers aligned at mid density, projection $D_{max}$ (4 to 5.5) scaled into the paper range.

### Dye Separation
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure.papers.resolve_saturation_matrix` / `compose_density_matrices`

Saturation in density, in a matrix stock's `DyeMatrix` slot: on $e = D - D_{min}$, $M(k) = k \cdot I + (1-k) \cdot J$, $J$ all $1/3$, so $k=1$ is identity, $k=0$ the achromatic mean, $k>1$ more separation. $M_{total} = M(k) \cdot M_{dye}$, saturation outermost so the dyes do not reabsorb it. On a LUT stock $M(k)$ runs alone before the LUT, so cleaner dyes give more chroma for the same $k$. $k$ (`dye_separation`, 0.5 to 1.5, default 1.0) takes per-channel trims. Rows sum to 1, so neutrals stay flat, and $k = 1$ everywhere returns `None`, keeping the default path byte-exact.

### Separation Damping
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure.logic.separation_damping_gain`

`separation_damping` ($\tau$, 0 to 1, default 0) makes $k$ depend on the pixel's color spread $c$ (`_rms_chroma`) about the mean $\bar e$:

$$h(c) = \frac{c_0 - c}{c_0 + c}, \qquad k_{eff,ch} = k_{ch}^{\,(1-\tau)\,+\,\tau\,h(c)}, \qquad e'_{ch} = \bar e + k_{eff,ch}\,(e_{ch} - \bar e)$$

At $\tau = 0$ this is the matrix above, byte-exact. At $\tau = 1$ the gain is $k$ near neutral, 1 at $c_0$ (`separation_damping_ref_spread` $= 0.35$, a WGSL literal) and $1/k$ far above it. Per-pixel $k$ cannot fold into a matrix, so with damping live `dye_mix` carries only the paper's coupling and the separation runs after it. It is monotone in $c$ for $k < e^2$, which covers the clamped $[0, 3]$ domain.

### Hue Trim (light-source hue rotation)
**Code**: `negpy.features.process.hue.apply_hue_trim`, mirrored in `exposure.wgsl`

A CIELAB hue rotation by $\theta$ (`process.hue_trim`, ±30°), $L^{\ast}$ and neutrals untouched:

$$\begin{pmatrix} a' \\ b' \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\ \sin\theta & \cos\theta \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \end{pmatrix}$$

It runs on the scene-linear print, after the curve and before the OETF, inside the exposure stage, and stays active under `RenderIntent.FLAT`. The GPU inlines `rgb_to_lab` / `lab_to_rgb` from `lab.wgsl` (WGSL has no includes). $\theta$ rides the x lane of `ExposureUniforms.hue`; yzw carry the preflash fraction, threshold value and paper gamma.

### Flat (log) master, "for editing elsewhere"
**Code**: `negpy.features.exposure.processor.PhotometricProcessor._process_flat` → `apply_flat_curve`

With `RenderIntent.FLAT`, a log encoding replaces the Print stage. The normalized $\text{val} \in [0,1]$ is already log, so it is emitted positive:

$$I_{out} = \text{clip}\big(\text{lift} + \text{gain} \cdot (1 - \text{val}),\ 0,\ 1\big)$$

with `flat_log_gain` $= 0.65$ and `flat_log_lift` $= 0.10$: shadow → $0.10$, mid-gray → $\approx 0.46$, highlight → $0.75$. Manual white balance applies as a log shift. A color print stock then mixes into $\text{val}$ as a density $3\times3$ $M$ with rows that sum to 1 (`resolve_flat_matrix`), so neutrals hold and the map stays log-linear. A flat master has no dye amounts for a LUT, so each LUT file carries a stand-in $M$: `build_print_luts.py` fits $10^{-Me}$ to the LUT in CIEDE2000 on dye amounts around the gray axis, and `--flat-only` refits it on the bundled tables. Only Geometry, Normalization, this map and Crop run. Light leak reduction still bakes in: a leak is a fault of the film, not a print decision. Export is full-resolution, 16-bit TIFF or lossless JPEG XL, on the CPU engine (there is no GPU flat shader).

### Transparency transfer, "the slide as captured"
**Code**: `negpy.features.transparency` (+ `shaders/transfer.wgsl`), `negpy.features.process.capture_color`

For Transparency and Positive a transfer curve replaces the Print stage, exactly the identity at default settings. `process.path.render_path` (`TRANSFER`, `POSITIVE`, `PRINT`) routes the base and exposure stages; the negative processors hold no slide branch. `TransparencyBaseProcessor` meters only for Auto Density and Auto Grade.

*   **Neutral decode**: this path always decodes without the as-shot white balance (`effective_linear_raw`), because the camera matrix folds it back in. Decode, both matrices and every cache token ask that helper; it also makes a TIFF load as literal linear data. Positive is exempt (`TiffLoader.load(positive_source=)`): its embedded profile decides, untagged falls back to sRGB.
*   **Highlight reconstruction** (`highlight_reconstruction`, `effective_highlight_reconstruction`): libraw `highlight_mode` `0` Clip, `2` Blend, `3` to `9` Reconstruct (the sidebar uses `5`). It is forced to `0` off Transparency, under Narrowband and on a merged bracket, whose weighting needs pixels near the sensor ceiling (`WorkspaceConfig.__post_init__`; `HdrWorker.run` zeroes it per frame). Active reconstruction bakes the real white balance and skips the matrix fold (`highlight_reconstruction_bakes_wb`, `should_fold_camera_wb`), because libraw finds clipping from its own gains; `highlight_reconstruction_bright_gain` passes the darkening ratio back as libraw's `bright`.
*   **Camera matrix**: libraw's `rgb_xyz_matrix` (`PipelineContext.cam_xyz`) becomes the working→camera matrix, row-normalized, then inverted (dcraw's `cam_xyz_coeff` order); normalizing after inversion gives a magenta cast. It applies in linear before the log. An explicit Input ICC swaps in `wb_only_cam_xyz` (`AppController._effective_cam_xyz`), which reduces to the white-balance fold.
*   **Fixed bounds**: a constant window from the decoder's white level ($D = 0$) to `TRANSFER_DENSITY_RANGE` $= 3.0$ decades below. White and Black Point (`per_channel_point_offsets`) shift it. No dye-crosstalk unmix.

`apply_transfer_curve` inverts the window and deviates only by what the user moved. With $n$ the normalized signal and $R$ the range:

$$D = R \cdot n - \Delta_{exp} + R \cdot \text{cmy} \qquad D \leftarrow p + (D - p) \cdot c$$
$$D \mathrel{+}= \text{cmy}_{sh} \cdot w_{sh} + \text{cmy}_{hl} \cdot (1 - w_{sh}) \qquad w_{sh} = \sigma\big(k_{wb}(D - z_{wb})\big)$$
$$D \leftarrow D - t \cdot \text{softplus}(D - k_t,\ w_t) + s \cdot \text{softplus}(k_s - D,\ w_s) \qquad I_{out} = \text{ODT}\big(2^{b} \cdot 10^{-D}\big)$$

At neutral $D = R \cdot n$, so $10^{-Rn}$ inverts the normalization exactly in float32. The ODT is a fixed baseline exposure $b$ = `transfer_baseline_ev` (0.7 EV, darktable's raw default) and Narkowicz's fit to the ACES RRT plus sRGB ODT; the decode anchors at sensor white (`no_auto_bright`). Positive skips both (the GPU packs `baseline_gain` 1.0, skip in `zone_taper.y`).

*   **Print Density** → $\Delta_{exp}$ (`transfer_density_stops` $= 2.0$ stops per unit). **ISO-R Grade** → $c$ = `transfer_grade_ref` / grade. **Toe** / **Shoulder** → $t, s$, knees $k_t = 1.6$, $k_s = 0.35$, widths about `transfer_width_ref` $= 2.5$. White balance → `filtration_offsets`, its regional split → `wb_split_geometry`. Zone Density and the regional term apply after contrast, before the knees. `transfer_grade_ref` and `transfer_width_ref` mirror `DEFAULT_WORKSPACE_CONFIG`, or the default stops being the identity (`test_transparency_transfer.py`).
*   **Auto Density / Auto Grade** (`transfer_auto_terms`) meter as on a negative, restated on this curve; `auto_meter_for_mode` starts them off on Slide and a live merge holds them off. The anchor sits at `transfer_contrast_pivot`; reach and holds map by tonal position.
*   **Zone geometry** maps from the print path's (`zone_geometry()`, `wb_split_geometry()`) by tonal position (shadow center 1.50 → 1.93). The shadow lift tapers to nothing at the window bottom (`_black_taper`, `ZONE_BLACK_TAPER` = 1.0), because no paper black stops it. Split Grade and the paper controls do not apply.
*   **Dye Separation** applies to density, $D'_{ch} = \bar D + k_{eff,ch}(D_{ch} - \bar D)$. Each channel enters $\bar D$ softly capped at the higher of 2.5 D and the lightest channel plus 1.5 D, set on the capture density, so a noise-dense channel near the window clamp does not spread. Separation Damping uses the shared chroma of the capped channels.

`linear_raw` and `sensor_matrix` are sticky global settings, inert where they do not apply. Linear RAW decodes with `user_wb=[1,1,1,1]`, and `camera_to_working_matrix` folds the green-normalized multipliers back in, so the render does not depend on the decode; with Positive on, Linear RAW decides the decode again. The sensor matrix is refused for E-6 on every path. The GPU mirror is a separate shader that emits display-encoded values; the CPU stays linear to the end of the engine.

### Linear Output
**Code**: `negpy.services.export.linear_output`

`RenderIntent.LINEAR` bypasses the darkroom pipeline: the native linear decode, lossless geometry (EXIF orientation, user rotation and flip) and a 16-bit TIFF or lossless JPEG XL (Effort 1 to 9, default 7). No normalization, exposure or color management runs. Only the TIFF is untagged: `_write_jxl()` pins `transfer=LINEAR` but `imagecodecs.jpegxl_encode()` cannot pass custom primaries, so JXL carries sRGB primaries; `JxlLoader` reads no color metadata, so NegPy re-imports it correctly.

*   **Pakon RAW**: expansion 4× for F135 (`PAKON_EXPANSION`), 1× for F335 (detected by file size); recorded in ImageDescription.
*   **LinearRaw DNG** (VueScan RGB+IR, SilverFast HDRi): read through tifffile, IR to an `_ir` file, expansion off. Only a `NewSubfileType` 0 page counts, so Adobe DNG Converter files are camera RAW. A 3-channel file libraw cannot unpack (DNG 1.7 JPEG-XL, [rawpy#207](https://github.com/letmaik/rawpy/issues/207)) falls back to tifffile, replaying `LinearizationTable`, black/white levels and the default crop (`_peek_linear_dng_rgb`), with only `AsShotNeutral` under Linear RAW.
*   **Camera RAW**: rawpy with `user_wb=[1,1,1,1]`, `output_color=raw`, `gamma=(1,1)`, `no_auto_bright=True`, demosaic from `resolve_demosaic`. As-shot WB goes into XMP as `RAW-WB: R G B`, the filename into `crs:RawFileName`.
*   **Coolscan NEF** (`nef_loader`): the largest RGB SubIFD (tag 0x014A); CFA SubIFDs are rejected.
*   **Flextight FFF** (`fff_loader`): 16-bit RGB in a top-level IFD by pixel count, or SGI LogLuv (tags 34676/34677) through `loaders/logluv` (from flexcolor-tool, MIT) to linear sRGB, normalized to the 0.2th/99.8th percentiles. Metadata from tags 50457 and 46279.
*   **Noritsu RAW** (`noritsu_loader`): headerless BGR 16-bit, sized from a table of known scan sizes, expansion 16× (`NORITSU_EXPANSION`).
*   **TIFF** (`_decode_tiff`): a 4th channel is IR only for ExtraSamples 0 or missing; `_ir.tif` sidecars and iSRD pages are detected. Input gamma (linear, 1.8, 2.2, sRGB) linearizes.
*   **Trichrome** merges through `merge_rgb_triplet()`, red primary; **stitches** assemble per-part corrected parts through `stitch_composite()`.

**Merge to TIFF Negative** (`services/export/frame_merge.py`) writes a triplet or stitch as one untagged 16-bit linear negative from the render decode (`ImageProcessor._load_source_f32`). A triplet decodes with flat-field off, so the gain stays live; a stitch keeps it on, because its registration holds only on corrected parts. `merged_edit` clears what was baked, `carry_edit` locks those cards against the roll, and `forget_composite` dissolves a stitch. Discovery never groups a merged file (`is_merged_source`). Brackets, transfer and positive frames (`needs_camera_matrix`) and sources whose format hides the assembly (`describes_a_merge`) are refused.

**Corrections** (all off): IR dust removal; for camera RAW, white balance, Flat Field and sensor correction, after EXIF orientation and before the user rotation, because the gain map is laid out on the EXIF-oriented decode (stitches always get Flat Field and sensor correction per part); Lens Correction on any source (embedded warp as in `prepare_lens_source`, manual `k1` after the user rotation; half frames skip it). `wb_bake_block_reason()` makes white balance inert on a triplet or a narrowband capture.

**Metadata**: raw pixels plus Make, Model and DateTime; source ICC and color tags are never copied. The description records source format, expansion, white balance and corrections and ends "no color management". `_write_jxl()` writes the same record into the JXL Exif and `xml ` boxes.

## 4. Local Contrast (CLAHE)
**Code**: `negpy.features.lab.logic.apply_clahe` (CPU) / `negpy/features/lab/shaders/clahe_{hist,cdf,apply}.wgsl` (GPU)

CLAHE on $L^{\ast}$ from linear working RGB; chroma is untouched. CPU and GPU mirror each other bin for bin.

*   **Tile grid**: a fixed $8\times8$ grid at every render scale, so the preview predicts the export; 256 bins over $L^{\ast} \in [0, 100]$.
*   **Clip limit**: $\max(1, \lfloor \text{strength} \cdot 2.5 \cdot N_{tile} / 256 \rfloor)$, the excess spread evenly, remainder to the lowest bins.
*   **Remap**: a smoothstep-weighted bilinear blend of the four nearest tile CDFs (centers at $(\text{pos}/\text{dims}) \cdot 8 - 0.5$, edge-clamped), then $L_{final} = (1 - \alpha) \cdot L + \alpha \cdot \text{CDF}(L) \cdot 100$, $\alpha$ = `lab.clahe_strength`.
*   **Tiled export**: every tile takes the meter render's CDF (`clahe_cdf_override`).

## 5. Retouching
**Code**: `negpy.features.retouch`

The IR channel, statistical detection and manual strokes each give one continuous defect score ($1$ clean, floor $0.02$), repaired by one shared fill plus a routed inpaint for defects too wide for it. Every repair is baked into the linear source before geometry and normalization, so the meters read cleaned film and CPU and GPU agree without implementing it.

*   **Light leak** (`leak_remove`, `features/leak/`; Print and Transfer paths, single frames only): the first bake, after flat-field and unmix, so every repair copies leak-free film. It reduces a leak only under the shapes in `leak_shapes`, placed by hand or by Auto. Every estimate runs on a 1024 px copy of the frame, so preview and export read the same leak.

    1.  **Model**: a leak is one light, so it adds exposure to the three emulsion layers in one ratio $k$, its color, times one strength map: $u_c = u_{scene,c} + A(s)\,F(t)\,k_c$. Each layer's density over the base $D$ (channel density through the crosstalk unmix when one is set) maps to excess exposure $u = 10^{D/\gamma} - 1$, $\gamma = 0.6$, the toe form $D = D_{min} + \gamma \log_{10}(1 + E)$. With **Film Dmax** $D_s$ set, the curve is $D = D_{min} + D_s \tanh(\gamma \log_{10}(1 + E) / D_s)$; its inverse stops at $0.995 D_s$, and a layer past that keeps its density. $D_s$ is never read from the frame, whose densest film is as often a bright subject as the film's ceiling, but never sits under that film's density (99.9th percentile per layer).
    2.  **Evidence**: the crop maps back through the geometry to a picture box. Each side with film outside it gives a rebate profile, the 95th-percentile transmittance of film pixels in 8 px bins of a strip up to 8% of the long side deep, so sprocket holes, bed and edge print drop out; $T_b$ is `film_base` refined to the thinnest bin. Rebate holds no scene, so its glow is the leak's own. In the picture, a leak reads in the **shadow spike**: the mode of the lowest quartile of $\log(1 + u)$ in 0.04 bins, where it holds 6% of the pixels. A leak only adds, in every layer, so a spike under the clean film's in any layer by more than 0.1 is a darker scene, not a reading.
    3.  **Color**: from lit rebate the shape crosses; else each layer's median ratio to the strongest over the cells that read strongest; else neutral. **Back** gives $k = (1, 0.2, 0.05)$, light through the base reaching the red-sensitive layer first; **Front** gives $(0.5, 0.8, 1)$, light on the emulsion side reaching the blue-sensitive layer first, as gate leaks on a color negative read. **Color Trim** $t$ scales the field in the layer $k$ leads in by $1 + t$. On B&W film every shape reads its color off the film, with no trim.
    4.  **Reduction**: the leak $L$ comes off the local mean $\bar u$ (a 1 px blur at the work size) through a softplus floor of width $0.1L$, and each pixel's $u$ takes the gain $g = \mathrm{floor}(\bar u - L)/\bar u$, so grain is scaled, not cut to bare base. A layer thinner than the base keeps its density, and a pixel nothing came off keeps its value. Shapes are read in order, each on what the ones before it left, and their fields add, so two over one leak remove it once.
    5.  **Band** (two ends of a line and a width each side, in long sides): $A(s)$ along the line times $F(t) = \cos^2(\tfrac{\pi}{2} t^{q})$ across, $t$ 0 on the line and 1 at either edge, $q = 4^{1 - 2f}$ at the shape's Falloff $f$, tapered past the line's ends over the mean width. In 24 px cells along the line, each side reads the spike within half the width less that of the clean film past its edge, divided by $F$ there; where both sides step darker, as a dark subject on the line with sky past the edges, the cell reads the cap. The readings project onto $k$ by least squares; a cell with no spike fits a straight scene ramp per layer plus $a F k$ with Tukey weights. A smooth curve (penalty $10^{-0.1}$ on second differences) through the readings, with Tukey's biweights, sets $A$; Strength scales it.
    6.  **Edge** (the ends of a stretch of picture edge and a reach $R_e$): $A(s)\,G(t / R(s))\,k$ at depth $t$, 1 outside the edge. Light through a gap spreads in the base and reaches further where it is stronger, so each 24 px cell has its own strength and reach, read from 12 depth bins of spikes less the clean film's past $R_e$. The fade is $G(x) = (e^{-3x} - e^{-3})/(1 - e^{-3})$, or, for the soft shadow of a gap at the gate, a shoulder $e^{-(3x)^2/2}$ eased to nothing at 1, taken where it fits the pooled readings twice as well.
        *   **Crossing**: a leak's light is continuous across the frame line. It crosses where the shadows right inside carry the rebate's light at the line at half to twice its level, in at least half the read cells, standing 1.75 times or more over the clean film's spike and at most 10 times the strip's thin end. A lit cell carries the glow where the picture's 5th percentile within 3 px inside is at 65% or more of the rebate's median within 3 px outside, in every layer the rebate shows; where fewer than half the lit cells do, the glow is fog or edge print, and an edge on Auto color reads no leak in the picture there.
        *   **Rebate profile**: with lit rebate, the leak follows its level $\ell(s)$ along the edge, the rebate's light within 3 px of the line interpolated over the lit cells (the strip's profile where fewer than two read it, or where that light is over 10 times the strip's thin end): $A = c\,\ell$ and $R = r\,(\ell/\ell_{max})^p$, $p \in \{0, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2\}$, fitted over every reading under their 25th percentile (pinball loss), as scene only adds; $c = 1$ where the leak crosses, else $c \le 1$. With no lit rebate, each cell fits its own strength and reach, and a cell with too few readings, as over smooth sky, takes them from its neighbors through the band's robust curve.
        *   **Steep tip**: rebate that rises at least 2 times over 0.6% of the long side away from the edge, lit at the line and carried by the picture's first row, is light piped along the base from the film's cut end. It is read per layer and per 1 px column: the rebate within 3 px of the edge is the strength, the fade $e^{-3x}$ eased to nothing at $x = 2$, its reach the shorter of the rebate's own and a fit under the picture's 25th percentile, and each layer's scale is capped per cell by the 10th percentile of the local mean over that strength.
        *   **Frame line inside a wide crop**: a crop set past the frame line holds rebate, and the edge reads from the line. Going in, film is rebate while its detail (the mean of the layers' $\log(1 + u)$ against its 3 px blur) stays under 1.5 times that of the rebate 4 to 8 px outside, starting within 1.67 times its level, up to 6% of the long side in. Where light-struck leader meets the gap, the step between them reads as texture too: smooth film within 20 px and 1.33 times dimmer is still rebate, and the search runs 6% on from there, to 12% at most. The frame line is the sharpest detail within 5 px of where it rises for two steps with the 10 px past it at twice the rebate's detail; under 5 px is no rebate. That rebate holds no scene, so along the whole side its width in from the line and out to the next frame's texture, read per 24 px cell and fitted as a straight line (cells off it by more than 3 px or twice the median offset set aside; the outer width is 0 where fewer than three cells read it), is cleared by a field of 1.5 times its local mean times $\min(\text{Strength}, 1)$. Where no crossing is proven, the clearing also needs the rebate lit (a median of 0.5) and the picture past it at 2.5 times the strip's lower-quartile detail, as a smooth stretch of picture reads as rebate too.
    7.  **Spot** (a center and the ends of two axes): $A\,F(\rho)\,k$, $\rho$ the distance from the center over the oval. In 8 sectors, the spike within half the oval is read against the ring past its edge as a band's sides are; the sectors' median is $A$, or the cap where no sector reads and one stepped. Shadows under a leak pile up at the leak itself, so where the core's 1st percentile of $u_c / F$ reads within 1.5 times that, its layers give $k$.
    8.  **Paint** (brush strokes): weight $W$, 1 inside, fading to 0 at the edge over the Falloff's share of its deepest point's depth, smoothstep. In 16 px cells, the spike inside is read against a 24 px ring of film around the region, carried in by normalized convolution over 3 cells; each layer's map is the lower envelope of its cells (an opening over 5 cells, a blur over 2), as a bright subject only lifts a cell. Its color is the 1st percentile of $u / W$ in the cells over half the strongest, where that is within 1.5 times the envelope, else the envelope's own layers.
    9.  **Caps**: along a band or edge, the field is capped by the 1st percentile of $u / (k F)$ near the line in the leak's strongest layer, smoothed over a cell in log and never above a cell's own reading; under a proven crossing, pixels under 0.25 in that layer are left out, as bare film there is rebate or a sprocket hole's rim. A spot caps by the same percentile in its core, a painted region per cell. Where a falloff reaches past its leak, a local cap keeps a dark subject from going to bare base: per 48-cell grid, the 1st percentile of each layer's $\bar u$, smoothed over 0.6 cells in log, joined by soft min. A steep tip and the rebate clearing are not held by it.
    10. **Recover Layers**: a layer inside the leak's area (over 10% of its peak), driven by its color (at least half its strongest layer's exposure) and within 5% of its ceiling (its shoulder with Film Dmax, else its 99.9th-percentile density; opened by 3 × 3) has lost its picture. Where another layer holds it, its $\log(1 + u)$ is rebuilt as $a\,g + b$ of the mean $g$ of the others, $a$ and $b$ a guided filter's local fit (ε 0.01) over film where the leak adds under 5%, in the smallest box reaching 3, 6, 12 or 24% of the long side each way where that film weighs 5%. Film saturated in every layer is marked for **Show Burned**.
    11. **Grain and Contrast**: where leak came off (a smoothstep of the luminance density removed over 0.05 D, blurred over 0.2% of the long side; never bed or sprocket hole), **Grain** blends toward He et al.'s guided filter of luminance density by itself (radius 0.06% of the long side, at least 2 px; $\varepsilon = (2\sigma)^2$, $\sigma$ the grain's robust spread) and **Contrast** adds up to 1.5 times the detail against a blur over 2% of the long side. The luminance shift scales all three channels.
    12. **Auto**: **bands** come from each 16 px cell's spike over the frame's 40th percentile, smoothed by 2 cells, and its Radon transform as line means every 2°. Of the 16 strongest lines, one is a leak when the bare rebate it crosses is lit over 7 bins by at least 2.0 and 6 of the side's spread, and the rebate 4 to 8 px outside the frame line by 2.0 too, since edge print never reaches the line; a line crossing no rebate must rank in the top 6, stand 6 robust deviations over its angle's lines and lift by 50. Lines within 35° and one width of a found band are the same leak; at most 3 are found. **Edges** read each picture side 0.3 long sides deep: cells where the leak crosses at 0.5 are strong, gaps of three cells join, and runs closer than the side's strong length merge into one edge where a run of three cells holds it, reaching its cells' 90th-percentile reach. A side with no such run whose crop holds lit rebate passing the no-crossing test above gets an edge 0.03 deep, which clears it. Auto places no spot or paint: with no rebate to vouch for it, a lamp or a lit window reads the same.
    13. **Slides** (Transfer path): a slide thins with exposure from its black, so a leak lowers its density. It is read as a negative, $T' = (T_f / T)^{0.6 / \gamma_s}$ per channel, $\gamma_s = 1.8$, $T_f = 10^{-5}$, so its black reads as base and clear film as the densest film. Everything above runs on $T'$, Film Dmax scaled by $0.6 / \gamma_s$, and the pixels removal moved convert back.
    14. **From Roll** (`leak/roll.py`) carries a frame's shapes to others by the picture box: each point keeps its place in the box, and each width and brush size its share of the box's long side. Nothing measured carries: each frame's bake reads the leak on its own film.

*   **Infrared dust removal** (Coolscan, SilverFast iSRD, VueScan DNG; concepts from digital-fauxice, `NOTICE.md`): dust blocks infrared and the dyes pass it. The IR plane follows the buffer being repaired, at most 1.5× under it and capped at 3600 px, because detection far below repair resolution inflates the mask. Footprints are pixels at a 1600 px reference and scale with the plane.
    1.  **Ratio**: $r = IR / \text{blur}(\text{dilate}(IR))$, about $1.0$ on clean film. IR more than 4σ over the clean median is glare and stays out of the base. A crosstalk fit first divides out the image's IR ghost. The clean floor and dip scale are stretched (never compressed) onto reference values, so a good scanner renders bit-identically.
    2.  **Division**: semi-transparent dust is divided out, $RGB / r^{\gamma}$ with per-channel γ fitted per frame, never past the local clean base (defect-excluded mean $- \sigma$).
    3.  **Fill**: $r$ maps to $s \in [0.02, 1]$ (IR Threshold moves the ramp), and cores are rebuilt as $\left(\sum I \cdot s \cdot w\right) / \left(\sum s \cdot w\right)$ over nested supports, finest clean support first: three rungs at the plane's footprint, one at the reference footprint for wide defects. The original-floor rule lets a repair only lighten, comparing the low-frequency deficit.
    4.  **Grain transplant**: detail high-passed from the nearest clean pixel is pasted back, mirrored through the donor so rows across a hair do not streak.
    5.  **Routed inpaint**: chebyshev radius ≥ 5 at detection scale goes to structure-following inpaint with the same grain, within a 2% frame budget.

    B&W silver and Kodachrome (the IR plane mirrors the image) are detected and skipped.
*   **ICE at scan time** (nkscan only): the driver's openICE port, baked into the file.
*   **Automatic dust removal** (`dust_remove`, Threshold, Size):
    1.  **Plane**: the source min-pooled to `ir_detect_target`, because an area average dilutes a two-pixel speck below the grain.
    2.  **Proxy**: percentile-normalized density ($-\log_{10}$ luminance, 0.5 to 99.5%), grade-independent.
    3.  **Excess**: over a background that is the max of a median ($3\times$ Size) and an opening (Size), averaged $3\times3$, divided by the local MAD σ ($4\times$ Size), on 8-bit planes. Windows scale with the plane.
    4.  **Gate**: Spot and Hair Threshold map to seed bars in σ (3 loosest, 9 default, geometric toward 48; 1.0 off), grown to a lower bar within $2\times$ Size. Compact components clear the spot bar, raised by the wide-window σ over a knee of 0.02; thin ones the hair bar, raised by the median σ along them over a knee of 0.075 and capped at twice the hair-filled σ. Stat maps are cached, so a Threshold change re-runs only the gate.
    5.  **Specks** go to the fill (released where IR Removal already repaired); **hairs** (thinness $\text{area}/\text{thickness}^2$) to Navier-Stokes inpaint, feathered over the PSF skirt.
    6.  **Exclusions**: right-painted strokes release the detections they cover, on the detection plane after the gate; the IR and manual routes are untouched. Strokes fold into the source token.
*   **Heal and Scratch**: a stroke is a search area. A two-sided $|z|$ (dust bright, scratch dark in density) against a local mean ($3\times$ the brush radius) over the stroke neighborhood's MAD σ seeds at 8 and grows to 2; a stroke that never reaches 8 seeds against its own maximum, and below 5 it does nothing. The fill runs with the original-floor rule off, so a scratch can darken, measuring clean fraction above the score floor. Each sparse defect is repaired in a padded crop with the whole frame's support ladder.
*   **Line tool**: a click seeds a nearly straight transport scratch, band-passed across it and normalized by local noise. The slope is fitted toward the click; extent and width are measured by the brush's hysteresis, so a stored line holds at any resolution. Line Sensitivity sets the ridge bar. There is no auto-detection, because a full-length ridge is as likely a horizon.
*   **Clone**: the last source bake. Each stroke stores raw-frame points, an offset, strength, feather and tone match; the patch is the shifted source in a capsule mask ramped over `feather` × the radius. Match Tone scales by $\bar d / \bar s$, Gaussian means outside the brush, clamped to ±6 stops.
*   **Coordinates**: relative to the full-resolution RAW, in raw-frame space, so rotation, flips and distortion correction need no mapping. A half frame reports its cropped full-resolution size even over a half-size decode.

## 6. Lab Scanner Mode
**Code**: `negpy.features.lab`

1.  **Chroma Denoise** (Fuji, US 5,739,922): $a^{\ast}b^{\ast}$ splits at a Gaussian of $\sigma$ = `chroma_denoise_sigma` (`gaussian_kernel_1d` taps on both engines). Detail above it survives only at edges, $ab' = \text{low} + k\,(ab - \text{low})$, $k = \text{smoothstep}(1.0, 2.5, \sigma\,|\nabla \text{low}|)$ (`CHROMA_EDGE_LO`/`HI`). L is untouched. Below $\sigma = 0.25$ px it is skipped.
2.  **Saturation**: an $a^{\ast}b^{\ast}$ scale. Above 1.0 it is gamut-aware (`gamut_aware_chroma_scale`, mirrored in `lab.wgsl`), because an RGB clamp shifts hue: pixels whose boosted color leaves $[0,1]$ (`_in_gamut_lab`) take a soft knee toward their own headroom, found by 10 bisection steps.
3.  **Skin Protection** (`skin_chroma_rein`, `lab.skin_protection`, default 0.5): a chroma ceiling in a skin mask (`_skin_weight`: hue $52°\pm20°$, full weight to $C^{\ast}=35$, zero by 60, rolloff below $L^{\ast}=15$ and above 95, zero under $C^{\ast}=2$). With mask $w$ and slider $s$:

    $$C_{\text{ceil}} = \frac{22}{s}, \qquad C_0 = 0.6\,C_{\text{ceil}}, \qquad C_{\text{knee}} = C_0 + (C_{\text{ceil}}-C_0)\left(1 - e^{-(C-C_0)/(C_{\text{ceil}}-C_0)}\right)$$

    for $C > C_0$, and $C_{\text{out}} = C + w\,(C_{\text{knee}} - C)$, hue and $L^{\ast}$ kept. It only reduces chroma, so the gamut knee's fit holds.
4.  **Sharpening**: Unsharp Mask or Deconvolution share Amount, Radius, Masking and the `gaussian_kernel_1d` taps (`sharpen_k`), so CPU (`cv2.sepFilter2D`) and GPU match bit for bit.

    $$L_{diff} = L - \text{blur}(L, \text{radius}), \qquad \text{gain} = \text{amount} \cdot 2.5 \cdot \text{smoothstep}(0.25, 0.33, |L_{diff}|) \cdot m \cdot g(L)$$
    $$L_{final} = \text{clamp}\big(L + L_{diff}\cdot\text{gain},\; L_{min}-2,\; L_{max}+1\big)$$

    Radius is in full-size pixels and scales down with a resized export. Masking $m = \text{smoothstep}(0.5t, t, |\nabla L|)$, $t = 10\cdot\text{masking}$. The shadow gain $g(L) = \tfrac{1}{3} + \tfrac{2}{3}\,\text{smoothstep}(0, 35, L)$ tames the grain of thin negatives (Gallagher & Gindele, US 7,228,004). The $3\times3$ clamp is tighter above, because $L^{\ast}$ USM exaggerates light halos.

    Deconvolution is Richardson-Lucy on linear $Y$ with a Gaussian PSF (`rl_*.wgsl`), $\text{clamp}(\text{round}(10\cdot\text{radius}), 5, 20)$ iterations, no damping:

    $$\hat{o}_{n+1} = \hat{o}_n \cdot \left(K \otimes \frac{o}{\max(K \otimes \hat{o}_n,\ \epsilon)}\right), \qquad \mathrm{RGB}_{out} = \mathrm{clamp}\left(\mathrm{RGB} \cdot \max\left(1 + \left(\frac{\hat{o}_N}{\max(o,\epsilon)} - 1\right) \cdot \mathrm{amount} \cdot m,\ 0\right),\ 0,\ 1\right)$$
5.  **Glow**: $I_{out} = I + s_{glow} \cdot \text{blur}(I \cdot m_{hl})$, $m_{hl}$ a display-domain ramp from 0.5 to 1.0, equal on all channels.
6.  **Halation**: $I_{out} = I + s_{hal} \cdot \text{blur}(I_R \cdot m_{lin} \cdot C_{hal})$, a wider blur, $m_{lin}$ a ramp on linear reflectance from 0.65 to 1.0 (so it follows scene exposure), tint $C_{hal} = (1.0,\ 0.3,\ 0.05)$.

## 7. Alternative Processes
**Code**: `negpy.features.lith`, `negpy.features.cyanotype`, `negpy.features.sabattier`; config `negpy.features.altprocess`

One process (B&W Negative only, `alt_process`, default none), between Lab and Toning so the toners act on it.

### 7.1 Lith

A low-gamma highlight branch fixed by exposure, then infectious development that drives the shadows to Dmax almost vertically (Rudman; Moersch). On print density $D_0$:

$$D_0' = D_0 + 0.301 E, \qquad D_h = f_{max}\left(1 - e^{-f_{rate} (D_0 + 0.301 E v) / f_{max}}\right)$$
$$D = D_h + (D_{max} - D_h) \cdot \sigma\!\left(\frac{D_0' - K}{w}\right)$$

*   **Exposure** $E$ (stops) moves the image up the axis; the highlight branch takes only `foot_veil` $v$ of it, so paper white does not fog.
*   **Snatch Point**: knee density $K = D_{max}(\text{knee}_{\text{lo}} - \text{knee}_{\text{span}}\cdot\text{snatch})$.
*   **Abruptness**: knee width $w$, down to about 0.03.
*   **Color**: the paper's $(a^{\ast}, b^{\ast})$ path on $u = D/D_{max}$ (`PaperProfile.lith_path`; Kong & Shore, 2007), anchors at $u = 0.10/0.35/0.65/1.00$: peach, ochre, olive, neutral. The olive knot is the green between warm highlights and cold blacks. $L^{\ast}$ comes from density.

Not modeled: diffusion halos, pepper fog, snowballs.

### 7.2 Cyanotype

No development: the sensitizer's density range is the contrast control (Ware). With exposure $E$ (stops) and scale $S$ (log D):

$$t = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{D_0 + 0.301 E}{S},\ 0,\ 1\right), \qquad v = 2t - 1, \qquad u_0 = (1-m)\,t + \tfrac{m}{2}\left(1 + v\,|v|\right)$$
$$u_b = u_0\left(1 - B\left(1 - \beta u_0\right)\right), \qquad u = u_b + T\left(r\,u_0 - u_b\right), \qquad D = D_{max}\,(1 + T g)\,u$$

*   Midtone compression $m = 0.45$, written $v|v|$ for engine parity, blended with a line so the center slope is $1-m$.
*   **Bleach** $B$ removes pigment highlights-first, leaving $\beta = 0.15$ of the deepest shadow. **Tannin** $T$ goes slightly past the blue ($r = 1.05$) with more covering power ($g = 0.15$).
*   **Color**: a path on $u$ with anchors at $0.00/0.15/0.55/1.00$ (rag white, green stain, mid blue, Prussian blue); tannin mixes toward iron tannate. Classic (Herschel) has $D_{max}$ 0.95, New (Ware) 1.40.
*   The chemical toners are skipped (no silver); split toning applies.

### 7.3 Sabattier

A re-exposure part-way through development: light tones reverse, folding at the re-exposure density, and restrained development along edges draws the Mackie line. With $R = \rho D_{max}$, fold width $w = 0.08$, edge width $w_e = 0.10$ (`SABATTIER_CONSTANTS`):

$$g = \sigma\!\left(\frac{D - R}{w_e}\right), \qquad D' = D + s\,\big(1 - \ell\,\text{blur}_{\sigma_m}(g)\big)\, w \ln\!\big(1 + e^{(R - D)/w}\big)$$

*   **Strength** $s$ (0 to 2, default 1.3): above 1 the light tones reverse; paper white returns at $sR$, clamped to $D_{max}$.
*   **Re-exposure** $\rho$ (0.1 to 0.9, default 0.45): the fold density.
*   **Agitation** $a$ (0 to 1, default 0.7): $\sigma_m = (1 - a)$% of the short side; at 1, $\ell = 0$. The blur uses `gaussian_kernel_1d` taps on both engines (`line_sigma_px`); a tiled export grows its halo by the kernel's half-width.
*   The print stays neutral silver, so the toners act on it.

## 8. Toning
**Code**: `negpy.features.toning`

*   **Chemical Toning** (B&W Negative only): Selenium, Sepia, Gold, Iron Blue, Copper and Vanadium as a silver ledger in density (`TONING_CONSTANTS`; Rudman, Ilford). The mean density $D_0$ is the reservoir.
    *   **Susceptibility** $c_i$ depends on $D_0$ only. Shadows first (Selenium, Iron Blue, Copper): $c = S \cdot (D_0/D_{ref})^{p}$, $D_{ref} = 0.9$ for the last two. Highlights first (Sepia, Gold, Vanadium): $c = S \cdot (1 - D_0/D_{ref})^{p}$. Strength above 1 is a longer bath; conversion caps at 1.
    *   **Ledger**: the untoned fraction $a$ starts at 1; in bath order each claims $f_i = a \cdot c_i$, $a \mathrel{-}= f_i$. Gold also plates the sepia fraction.
    *   **Covering power**: $D_{ch} = D_{0,ch} \cdot \left(a + \sum_i f_i \cdot g_{i,ch}\right)$, $I_{out} = 10^{-D_{ch}}$, per channel, so existing color survives. Gains: selenium all $\ge 1$; sepia lower; gold slight intensification; Prussian blue net $> 1$ with G exactly $1.00$ (sepia-plus-blue splits green); copper net $< 1$; vanadium absorbs R/B with slight loss.
    *   **On a lith print** (`LITH_TONING_CONSTANTS`): Selenium uses $D_{ref} = 1.2$, exponent $1.0$ and a green-led gain that turns green-black shadows magenta (Moersch); Gold is flat $c = S$, blue-violet.
*   **Split Toning** (all modes): $a^{\ast} \mathrel{+}= \cos\theta \cdot 20 \cdot S \cdot m$, $b^{\ast} \mathrel{+}= \sin\theta \cdot 20 \cdot S \cdot m$, with $m_{shadow} = 1 - \text{smoothstep}(0, 50, L)$ and $m_{highlight} = \text{smoothstep}(50, 100, L)$.

## 9. Finish
**Code**: `negpy.features.finish`

Post-crop finishing in scene-linear, before the output transform: edge burn, then filed carrier; layout extras run at compositing. The uncropped tool previews skip the carrier and the layout on both engines.

*   **Edge Burn**: $I_{out} = I \cdot 2^{-s \cdot m}$, $s$ in stops, $m$ a cosine falloff from radial to rectangular (Roundness) with its midpoint at Size.
*   **Filed Carrier**: the rebate prints between the camera gate and the filed aperture, framed by an unexposed margin ($0.7 \cdot w$) in the mat color (`PrintService.effective_paper_linear`). `rebate_tone` pushes a density ramp from the film base (thin bound plus `REBATE_BASE_MARGIN` $= 0.15$) through the frame's exposure kernel, saturation and toning into a 64-entry table over exposure $t = u^3$, divided by its $t = 0$ entry, so bare paper meets the mat exactly. With Paper Black on, the rebate stops at the paper's D-max. Alternative processes, slides and flat masters use $1 - t$. Both engines read the one table, so the shader has no print model.

    The gate only wobbles (`carrier_profiles()` rows 0 to 3) with a small corner radius; Roughness swings the aperture off rows 4 to 7, and Corners rounds only the aperture, offset by `CARRIER_OFFSET_X/Y` of $w$, so the rebate is wider at the top and left. The aperture also gates the picture (weight $A_{in} A_{out}$). A hash-value fBm (`carrier_noise`, u32 wrap-around) displaces both edges, so WGSL matches bit for bit. Flare adds exposure on each filed edge over $0.25 \cdot w$, gated by the other edges. Profiles are fixed-seed, so one carrier prints the whole roll.
*   **Layout extras** (`services/export/print.py`, `layout.wgsl`): the bottom-weighted mat and match paper white through the toning stack.
*   **Export sharpening** (`apply_resize_sharpening`): a Print or Pixels export runs the Lab unsharp mask on the scaled content at `RESIZE_SHARPEN_RADIUS`, on the CPU after either engine.
*   **Preview paper size**: an interactive render sizes the paper from `preview_render_size`, never resampling above the content's own resolution, because the canvas quotes zoom against the pipeline buffer.
